Morning Practice

The Four Immeasurables

Catvāri Brahmavihārāḥ · The Four Abodes of the Awakened Heart

Begin the day here. Move through it slowly, in order — not reading the words, but generating each quality until it is actually felt, then letting it radiate outward.

i
SettleThe Good Beginning · Body

Sit upright and at ease. Let the weight of the body drop into the ground. Take three slow breaths, and with each out-breath let the night's residue leave the body.

Arrive fully before you begin. There is nowhere else to be.

ii
Opening PrayerRefuge & Bodhicitta · Speech

Bring Guru Rinpoche to mind, present before you. Then recite, slowly:

In the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Supreme Assembly, I take refuge until enlightenment is reached. By the merit of generosity and the other perfections, may I awaken fully for the benefit of all beings.

Traditionally recited three times.

As you say the first two lines, feel yourself turning toward what is reliable — placing your trust in the awakened ones, the teaching, and the community. As you say the last two lines, let the aim open outward: this practice is not for myself alone, but so that I may be of use to every being.

The Good Middle

Take each of the four in turn. Feel it first for yourself, then let it widen — circle by circle — until it holds all beings.

How to Radiate Each One

Yourself — begin where warmth is easiest to find
Someone dear — a loved one, easy to wish well
A neutral person — someone you barely notice
A difficult person — one you're in friction with
All beings — everywhere, without exception
Maitrī
Loving-Kindness
May I be safe, well, and at peace. May those I love know genuine happiness. May all beings enjoy happiness and its true causes.
The real thing: warmth that wants another's happiness. Its counterfeit is attachment — wanting them happy so I feel good. Rest in the open wish, not the grasp.
Karuṇā
Compassion
May I be free from anxiety, tension, and pain. May those who struggle find comfort and relief. May all beings be free from suffering and its causes.
The real thing: the heart trembling with another, as an equal. Its counterfeit is pity — feeling above them. Meet suffering level-eyed, not from a height.
Muditā
Sympathetic Joy
May I delight in my own good fortune without clinging. May I rejoice in others' happiness without envy. May the joy of all beings continue to grow.
The real thing: gladness at another's success as if it were your own. Its counterfeit is comparison. This is the direct antidote to the old habit of measuring yourself against the field.
Upekṣā
Equanimity
May I meet whatever arises — pleasant or unpleasant — with a steady heart. May I rest in clarity, free from grasping and aversion. May all beings abide in the great evenness, beyond near and far.
The real thing: a calm, caring steadiness that holds friend and stranger equally. Its counterfeit is indifference — cold, not clear. Even, because you care about all beings equally, not because you've stopped caring.
iii
Rest in AwarenessThe Afterglow · Dissolve

Let the words fall away. Let even the wish dissolve. Drop the project entirely and rest in open, spacious awareness — the ground the warmth arose from.

The Immeasurables warm the heart. Resting lets it settle back into rigpa.

iv
Closing DedicationThe Good End · Seal

Do not keep the merit. Give it away completely — hold nothing back for yourself. Recite:

By this merit, may all beings complete the accumulation of merit and wisdom, and attain the two supreme kāyas that arise from merit and wisdom.

Then, in your own words:

May whatever goodness arose here ripen for every being. May all recognize their own nature and be free from suffering.

Then rise, and carry the open heart into the day.

Oṃ Āḥ Hūṃ

Settle · take refuge and rouse bodhicitta · generate and radiate each of the four · rest in the afterglow · dedicate. You already do the middle. This is only the frame around it.
